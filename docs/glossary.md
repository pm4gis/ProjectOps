\# Glossary



Short definitions used across ProjectOps. Keep these consistent so teams do not talk past each other.



\## Adoption

People and teams using the change in real work, with capability and confidence.



\## Assumption

Something treated as true for planning purposes, without proof. Assumptions should be recorded and reviewed.



\## Baseline

A starting measure recorded before change, used to compare results after release.



\## Benefit

A measurable improvement expected from the change, owned by someone accountable for tracking it.



\## Benefit owner

The person accountable for confirming that a benefit was achieved, using agreed measures and timeframes.



\## Change impact

What will be different for a person, team, customer, or system, including effort, process changes, and risks.



\## Change readiness

Evidence that users, support teams, and operational owners are prepared for release.



\## Constraint

A limit that shapes delivery, such as time, budget, policy, technology, or capability.



\## Decision log

A record of key decisions including date, decision maker, context, rationale, and what the decision affects.



\## Definition of done

The conditions that must be met for work to be considered complete, including quality and acceptance criteria.



\## Dependency

Something that must be delivered, decided, or available before work can proceed safely.



\## Epic

A larger piece of work that is broken into smaller items (stories, tasks) that can be delivered and tested.



\## Governance

The decision-making structure that controls prioritisation, risk appetite, scope, and accountability.



\## Handover

The transfer of knowledge and ownership from delivery to operations and support, including documentation and readiness.



\## Implementation

Putting the change into the target environment, process, or operating context.



\## Issue

A current problem that is already affecting delivery or outcomes, with an owner and an action plan.



\## Milestone

A meaningful point in the plan that signals a capability, decision, or release is completed.



\## Outcome

A real-world result, not just an output. Outcomes should be measurable.



\## Output

A deliverable produced by the project, such as a feature, system, process, or training material.



\## Portfolio

A collection of initiatives managed together to optimise prioritisation, sequencing, and value.



\## Prioritisation

Deciding what work should be done first, based on value, risk, dependencies, and capacity.



\## RAID

Risks, assumptions, issues, and dependencies, tracked with owners and regular review.



\## Readiness checklist

A short list of checks used before release, covering business readiness, technical readiness, support, and comms.



\## Release

A packaged set of changes delivered into use, with a defined scope and readiness checks.



\## Requirement

A needed capability or constraint. Requirements should be small, testable, and linked to outcomes.



\## Risk

A potential future problem that could affect delivery or outcomes, with likelihood, impact, and mitigation.



\## Scope

What is included in the work, and what is explicitly not included.



\## Scope change

A documented change to what is included, often requiring a decision, impact assessment, and plan update.



\## Stakeholder

A person or group affected by the change, able to influence it, or required to support it.



\## Traceability

The ability to follow a line from outcome to requirement to delivery item to release, and back again.



\## Value

The usefulness and impact of the change, including financial, risk, service, customer, and capability measures.
