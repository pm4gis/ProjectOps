# Lifecycle

ProjectOps treats delivery as a connected lifecycle rather than a set of hand-offs. The point is to keep intent, decisions, and readiness connected from the first idea through to measurable outcomes.

This page describes a practical lifecycle you can use with agile, waterfall, or a mix.

## Lifecycle at a glance

**Idea → Plan → Deliver → Adopt → Measure → Learn**

Each step produces a small set of artefacts that stay linked as the work progresses.

## 1. Idea

Purpose:
- clarify why the work matters
- define the outcome you want in the real world
- confirm ownership and appetite to proceed

Typical activities:
- problem statement and context
- outcome definition and success measures
- initial options and trade-offs
- rough sizing and constraints

Suggested artefacts:
- one-page project brief (draft)
- initial outcomes and measures
- initial stakeholder list
- known constraints and assumptions

Exit checks:
- someone is accountable for the outcome
- success measures exist, even if rough
- there is agreement to invest in planning

## 2. Plan

Purpose:
- turn intent into a realistic sequence of work
- make dependencies visible early
- define scope boundaries and decision points

Typical activities:
- scope and boundaries (in and out)
- dependency mapping
- delivery approach (iterations, milestones, releases)
- requirements shaping at the right level of detail
- risk and issue set-up

Suggested artefacts:
- project brief (approved)
- delivery plan (iterations, milestones, or hybrid)
- dependency list or map
- RAID log with owners and review cadence
- decision log started from day one
- initial change impact assessment

Exit checks:
- the plan matches capacity and constraints
- dependencies have owners and dates
- risks are visible and actively owned
- change and readiness work is scheduled, not “later”

## 3. Deliver

Purpose:
- build or implement the change with quality and visibility
- keep scope, decisions, and risks under control
- prepare for release as you deliver, not at the end

Typical activities:
- backlog management and delivery execution
- quality controls and acceptance checks
- technical and operational readiness work
- ongoing stakeholder engagement and comms
- managing scope change through explicit decisions

Suggested artefacts:
- delivery board or work queue
- acceptance criteria and definition of done
- release plan and release notes
- updated RAID log
- updated decision log
- updated requirements and traceability

Exit checks:
- work is releasable, not just “mostly done”
- support and operational owners are ready
- user communications and training are ready or scheduled
- known issues are accepted or resolved with owners

## 4. Adopt

Purpose:
- ensure people can and will use the change
- manage readiness and support through go-live
- track early signals and respond quickly

Typical activities:
- training delivery
- communications and stakeholder support
- go-live support and triage
- operational handover and ownership confirmation
- adoption monitoring

Suggested artefacts:
- readiness checklist (signed off)
- comms plan and messages delivered
- training plan and completion evidence
- support plan and escalation paths
- known issues list and resolution plan

Exit checks:
- users are enabled and supported
- operational ownership is clear
- early adoption signals are tracked

## 5. Measure

Purpose:
- confirm whether the change produced the intended outcomes
- measure benefits with evidence
- feed results back into prioritisation and future work

Typical activities:
- capture outcome measures against baselines
- benefits register updates
- lessons learned and improvement actions
- decide whether follow-up work is needed

Suggested artefacts:
- benefits register with dates and owners
- baseline and after measures
- outcome review notes (30, 60, 90 days is common)
- support trend summary (tickets, incidents, user feedback)

Exit checks:
- outcomes are assessed, not assumed
- benefits are recorded with evidence
- follow-up actions are owned and scheduled

## 6. Learn

Purpose:
- improve the operating model over time
- keep knowledge usable for future work
- reduce repeated mistakes

Typical activities:
- retrospective or review session
- documentation tidy-up (what should remain, what should not)
- update templates and checklists
- update governance rules if needed

Suggested artefacts:
- lessons learned summary
- updated templates and playbooks
- decision record for process changes

Exit checks:
- improvements are captured and adopted
- learnings are linked to the work that generated them

## Common variants

### Agile delivery
- Plan is lighter and continuous.
- Deliver and Adopt happen in short cycles.
- Measure happens per release or per quarter.

### Waterfall or stage-gate
- Plan is heavier up front.
- Deliver is structured into phases.
- Adopt and Measure still need explicit artefacts and owners, not a handover email.

### Hybrid
- Use milestones for governance and funding.
- Use iterations for delivery flow.
- Keep ChangeOps and ValueOps running throughout.

## Minimum viable lifecycle

If you want the smallest useful set, keep these always:
- project brief with outcome measures and owners
- decision log
- RAID log
- readiness checklist before release
- one outcome review after release
