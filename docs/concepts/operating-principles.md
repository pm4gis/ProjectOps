# Operating principles

ProjectOps is designed to be practical. These principles help you make consistent choices across tools, methods, and team structures.

## 1. Outcomes first

Define success in real-world terms:
- what will be different
- how you will measure it
- who is accountable

If you cannot measure it, you can still deliver it, but you should be honest about uncertainty.

## 2. One system, not separate streams

Planning, delivery, change, and benefits should not live as separate plans with different owners and dates. Keep a connected set of artefacts and routines so work stays aligned.

## 3. Small artefacts, kept current

Prefer short, maintained documents over long documents that go stale.

Good examples:
- one-page brief
- decision log
- readiness checklist
- benefits register

Avoid:
- large documents that are written once and never used again

## 4. Decisions must be visible

If a decision matters, record it:
- what was decided
- why
- who decided
- what it affects

This reduces repeated debate and supports governance without heavy ceremony.

## 5. Change is part of delivery

A release that users cannot adopt is not complete. ChangeOps should run alongside delivery, with readiness checks before go-live.

## 6. Make dependencies explicit early

Hidden dependencies are a common cause of delay. Identify them early and assign owners.

If you cannot resolve a dependency, make it visible as a risk and plan around it.

## 7. Reduce rework by clarifying “done”

Work should have clear acceptance criteria and a shared definition of done. This improves quality and reduces late-stage arguments.

## 8. Keep ownership clear

Every critical item needs a named owner:
- outcomes and benefits
- risks and issues
- decisions
- readiness

Shared ownership often becomes no ownership.

## 9. Use feedback loops, not status theatre

Meetings should change decisions or reduce risk. Prefer:
- short reviews that unblock work
- readiness checks that prevent failure
- outcome reviews that confirm value

Avoid repeating status that is already visible in a board or dashboard.

## 10. Tool-agnostic, evidence-led

ProjectOps can be run across many tools. Choose tools that make evidence easy to maintain:
- traceability from outcome to work items
- simple reporting
- easy access for the people who need to use it

## Practical application

When you are unsure what to do next, ask:

- What outcome are we trying to achieve?
- What is the smallest next step that reduces risk?
- What decision do we need to record?
- Who owns this item?
- What needs to be ready before release?
- How will we confirm value after release?
