# PlanningOps

PlanningOps keeps priorities, scope, and sequencing clear. It creates a plan that delivery teams can actually execute and that stakeholders can trust.

PlanningOps is not “write a plan and file it”. It is continuous planning that stays connected to delivery reality.

## What PlanningOps is responsible for

- the outcome and success measures are clear
- scope is explicit, including what is out of scope
- dependencies are visible and owned
- the delivery approach matches constraints and capacity
- decision points are clear and scheduled
- changes to scope are controlled through decisions

## Inputs

Typical inputs include:
- strategy, policy, and regulatory constraints
- customer or user needs
- operational constraints (support capacity, change windows, environments)
- technical constraints (architecture, security, data, integrations)
- current portfolio commitments and capacity

## Key artefacts

### 1) Project brief (one page)

Minimum recommended fields:
- problem statement
- outcome statement and measures
- scope boundaries (in and out)
- key stakeholders and owners
- major risks and constraints
- delivery approach (iterations, milestones, or hybrid)
- target release window and success criteria

### 2) Work breakdown

Choose one of:
- backlog (epics, stories, tasks), or
- milestone plan (work packages), or
- both (hybrid)

Rule of thumb: items should be small enough to be delivered and accepted without weeks of uncertainty.

### 3) Dependency map

Keep a simple dependency list if a map is too much.

Minimum fields:
- dependency description
- owner
- due date
- impact if late
- mitigation or alternate path

### 4) RAID log

Keep it live and owned. Do not treat it as a reporting form.

### 5) Decision points

Identify where decisions are required, for example:
- funding or scope approval
- design approval
- release approval
- risk acceptance decisions

## Routines

### Weekly planning and delivery alignment

Agenda:
- what changed since last week
- new dependencies and constraints
- scope changes requiring a decision
- upcoming decision points

### Monthly prioritisation review

Agenda:
- what is in progress, what is stalled, what is at risk
- value and urgency changes
- capacity and sequencing changes
- decisions required to keep flow healthy

## Planning checklists

### Planning readiness checklist

Before committing to delivery, confirm:
- outcome and measures exist
- owners are named
- scope boundaries are agreed
- major dependencies are identified and owned
- risks are visible and reviewed
- change impacts are at least initially assessed

### Scope change checklist

When scope changes, record:
- what changed (in or out)
- why (new information, constraint, decision)
- impact on timeline, cost, risk, readiness, benefits
- who approved
