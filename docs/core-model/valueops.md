# ValueOps

ValueOps defines what success means, measures it, and confirms whether outcomes and benefits were achieved. It keeps delivery focused on value rather than activity.

ValueOps is not a reporting exercise at the end. It starts at intake and continues after release.

## What ValueOps is responsible for

- outcomes and success measures are clear and agreed
- baselines are captured where possible
- benefit ownership is explicit
- measurement is practical and scheduled
- outcomes are reviewed after release
- evidence exists for governance and investment decisions

## Core concepts

### Outcome vs output

- Output: what you deliver (feature, system, process, training)
- Outcome: what changes in the real world (faster service, fewer errors, reduced risk)

ValueOps focuses on outcomes and links outputs to those outcomes.

## Key artefacts

### 1) Benefits register

Minimum fields:
- benefit statement
- measure and data source
- baseline
- target
- benefit owner
- review dates
- status and evidence link

### 2) Measurement plan

Keep it simple:
- what will be measured
- where data comes from
- who captures it
- when it will be reviewed
- how it will be reported

### 3) Outcome review notes

Use a repeatable structure:
- what was expected
- what happened
- what evidence supports this
- what follow-up is required
- what learnings should feed into future work

## Routines

### Monthly value review

Agenda:
- benefits status changes
- risks to benefits (not only delivery risks)
- data quality issues
- decisions needed (extend, stop, reshape)

### Post-release outcome checks

Common cadence:
- 30 days
- 60 days
- 90 days

Choose fewer if you want to start small.

## Practical measurement ideas

Examples:
- cycle time or time-to-service reduction
- defect or error rate reduction
- customer satisfaction change
- compliance or risk reduction evidence
- support volume trends
- adoption and usage trends
