# ChangeOps

ChangeOps makes sure people can and will use what you deliver. It integrates stakeholder engagement, communications, training, readiness, and early-life support into the delivery flow.

ChangeOps is not a late-stage comms activity. It runs throughout the lifecycle and aligns with releases.

## What ChangeOps is responsible for

- stakeholders understand what is changing and why
- impacts are identified early and reviewed as scope evolves
- communications are timely and consistent
- training and support are planned and delivered
- readiness is checked before each release
- adoption signals are monitored after go-live

## Key artefacts

### 1) Stakeholder map

Minimum fields:
- stakeholder group
- influence and interest
- impact level
- preferred channels
- engagement cadence
- owner

### 2) Change impact assessment

Cover:
- process changes
- role changes and capability needs
- system changes and access changes
- policy or compliance impacts
- operational impacts (support load, escalations, after-hours)

### 3) Communications plan

Keep it practical:
- who needs to know
- what they need to know
- when they need to know it
- who sends it
- how feedback is captured

### 4) Training plan

Define:
- audiences and required capability
- training formats (guides, videos, workshops, drop-ins)
- timing aligned to release plan
- completion evidence or sign-off where needed

### 5) Readiness checklist

Readiness is evidence. Common checks:
- comms sent and understood
- training delivered or scheduled
- support model ready
- access and permissions confirmed
- operational owners prepared for early-life support

## Routines

### Stakeholder cadence

Typical options:
- weekly for high impact programmes
- fortnightly for medium impact
- monthly for low impact

### Readiness review before release

Run as a short decision meeting:
- what is ready
- what is not ready
- what is the mitigation
- do we proceed, delay, or reduce scope

### Early-life support review

Within 1 to 2 weeks after release:
- what issues occurred
- what adoption signals look like
- what should change in comms, training, or support

## Practical adoption measures

Choose a small set relevant to the change:
- usage metrics (logins, feature use, completion rates)
- support volume and themes
- user feedback themes
- time saved or error rate reduction (where relevant)
