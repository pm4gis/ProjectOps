# KnowledgeOps

KnowledgeOps keeps decisions, requirements, and learning usable across the lifecycle. It reduces repeated debates, supports onboarding, and makes governance easier without heavy paperwork.

KnowledgeOps is not “write more documentation”. It is “keep the right knowledge current and findable”.

## What KnowledgeOps is responsible for

- decisions are recorded and easy to find
- requirements are traceable to outcomes and work items
- key artefacts have a single source of truth
- knowledge supports operations and support after release
- learning is captured and applied

## Key artefacts

### 1) Decision log

Minimum fields:
- decision statement
- date
- decision maker(s)
- context and options considered
- rationale
- impacts and follow-up actions
- links to affected work items

### 2) Requirements catalogue (lightweight)

Do not over-engineer it. Good requirements are:
- small
- testable
- linked to outcomes
- linked to acceptance criteria

If you use a ticket system, the ticket fields and links may be your catalogue.

### 3) Change history and scope changes

Keep a simple record of scope changes:
- what changed
- why
- who approved
- impacts on timeline, risk, readiness, benefits

### 4) Lessons learned

Keep it practical:
- what happened
- what we will do next time
- what we will stop doing
- what we will keep doing
- owners for improvement actions

## Routines

### Decision logging as you go

If a decision is discussed, record it while it is fresh. Waiting until the end creates gaps.

### Monthly knowledge tidy-up

A short review:
- remove dead links
- ensure key pages point to the current source of truth
- confirm operational handover artefacts exist

### Post-incident and post-release write-ups

Short write-ups create high value learning. Keep them blame-free and action-focused.

## Practical structure (recommended)

Maintain links between:
- outcome statement
- requirements and acceptance criteria
- work items
- release notes
- readiness checklist
- outcome review evidence

This makes audits, reviews, and onboarding much easier.
